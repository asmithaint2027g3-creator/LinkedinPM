## Complete LinkedIn Post Copy

Not all revenue is created equal. $10M in ARR at a 55% gross margin is worth less to enterprise valuation than $6M in ARR at an 85% margin.

In the growth-at-all-costs era, companies celebrated every signed contract:
- Sales reps were incentivized purely on top-line booking volume.
- Deals requiring heavy professional services and custom engineering were accepted gladly.
- The company grew top-line revenue by 40%, but operating cash flow turned negative.

When you examine the unit economics, low-quality revenue is a financial trap:
- It requires 3x the customer support headcount.
- It consumes scarce engineering capacity on bespoke integrations.
- It churns at double the rate of core ICP accounts when multi-year renewals come up.

High-yield enterprises obsess over Revenue Quality:
1. High Gross Margin Profile: Contracts that rely on standard, repeatable software architecture rather than manual human services.
2. Low Cost-to-Serve: Customers whose technical maturity enables fast time-to-value with minimal onboarding drag.
3. Natural Expansion Vector: Accounts positioned to expand their contract size organically as their business scales.

Stop celebrating vanity ARR milestones. Measure the gross margin yield of every signed agreement.

How much of your current pipeline consists of high-maintenance, margin-diluting revenue?
