## Complete LinkedIn Post Copy

The sales forecast delivered to the CFO is almost always a work of creative fiction.

Every quarter, the same ritual plays out:
- Sales leadership submits a forecast predicting $5M in new ARR.
- The CFO senses that the numbers are inflated but lacks empirical telemetry to challenge individual deal assumptions.
- So finance applies an arbitrary 'haircut': 'Let's discount their number by 20% to be safe.'
- The company budgets based on that estimate.
- At quarter-end, actual revenue comes in at $3.2M—missing even the discounted projection.

CFOs can no longer afford to manage corporate liquidity using guesswork and arbitrary haircuts.

Empirical Pipeline Stress-Testing:
1. Behavioral Audit Over Stage Probabilities: Disregard CRM stage percentages. Inspect behavioral telemetry: Has the buyer signed a mutual action plan? Has the InfoSec questionnaire been completed?
2. Historical Dwell-Time Discounting: Automatically write down the forecast value of any deal that has languished in a stage past its historical median.
3. Single-Threaded Risk Penalties: Discount deals by 50% if the sales team has only engaged a single non-budget-holding champion.
4. Closed-Lost Cohort Realism: Measure historical slippage rates by deal size and apply empirical loss curves to the active pipeline.

When finance stress-tests pipeline data using empirical telemetry, forecast variance drops to under 5%, protecting corporate capital allocation.
