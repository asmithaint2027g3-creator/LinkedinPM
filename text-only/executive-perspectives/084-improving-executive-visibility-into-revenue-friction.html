Adding 20 sales reps to an unproven or inefficient go-to-market motion does not produce more revenue. It produces a massive quarterly loss.

Many CEOs fall into the premature scaling trap:
- They close three large enterprise deals.
- They assume product-market fit is solved and tell the board they are ready to scale.
- They hire 15 new account executives.
- Six months later, new reps have near-empty calendars, quota attainment drops to 30%, and cash burn triples.

Scaling sales capacity should never be based on executive optimism. It must be gated by empirical unit economics:

The 4 Non-Negotiable Gates for Scaling Sales Capacity:
1. Cohort Quota Consistency: Are at least 70% of your existing, ramped account executives consistently meeting or exceeding their assigned quotas? (If only your top 2 reps are hitting, you have rep heroics, not a repeatable system).
2. Predictable Inbound & Intent Flow: Does the revenue engine generate enough verified ICP demand to fill 70%+ of each new rep's pipeline capacity without reps cold-calling random phone directories?
3. Stable Win Rates & Dwell Velocity: Are win rates holding steady above 25% with sales cycle duration within historical bounds?
4. Sub-12-Month CAC Payback: Does the gross margin generated by new cohorts fully recover their acquisition cost within one year?

If you cannot check all four gates, scaling headcount will dilute capital.

Fix your commercial system first. Then pour fuel on the fire.
