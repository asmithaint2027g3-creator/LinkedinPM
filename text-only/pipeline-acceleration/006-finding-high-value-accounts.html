A Fortune 500 logo on your target account list is a liability if you don't have an economic entry point.

Many enterprise sales teams build target lists consisting entirely of recognizable household brands. Twelve months later:
- The team has spent 1,000 hours knocking on impenetrable security walls.
- Zero deals have entered the proposal stage.
- Quota capacity has been wasted while agile competitors win high-yield mid-market and upper-tier contracts.

High-value account prioritization is not an exercise in prestige. It is an exercise in commercial math.

Evaluate your target accounts through three pragmatic filters:

  1. Latent Value Yield: Does this organization have multiple operational business units that can expand contract size beyond initial land ARR?
  2. Technical & Process Friction: Will their internal procurement and security review take 14 months, or do they possess modern architectural standards that accelerate deployment?
  3. Verified Economic Accessibility: Can your commercial team identify the P&L owner who is currently held accountable for the specific metric you impact?

If an account scores 10/10 on revenue size but 2/10 on accessibility and execution readiness, deprioritize it.

Focus your top account executives where value potential and commercial momentum intersect.
