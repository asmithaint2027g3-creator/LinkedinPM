Deals do not slip overnight. They give off silent warning signals for 3 weeks before they miss their close date.

When a sales rep suddenly announces on Friday afternoon that a committed $150K deal is pushing to next quarter, leadership is blindsided.

But when you inspect the deal's digital telemetry, the collapse was completely predictable:
- Email response time from the champion increased from 2 hours to 48 hours.
- The CFO was invited to three meetings but never attended.
- The prospect's weekly logins to the mutual evaluation workspace dropped to zero.
- The security questionnaire was sent 20 days ago and hasn't been opened.

Autonomous Deal Risk Detection identifies these silent decay signals while there is still time to intervene:

  1. Communication Latency Surges: Automated alerts when buyer response intervals double compared to the deal's historical baseline.
  2. Buying Committee Absence: Flagging deals in Stage 3+ where an economic buyer or legal stakeholder has not confirmed attendance.
  3. Dwell-Time Limit Violations: Warning managers when an opportunity exceeds the historical median duration for that stage.
  4. Sentiment Shifts: Detecting subtle language changes in prospect emails (e.g., shifting from 'when we implement' to 'if this gets approved').

When risk alerts trigger automatically, sales leaders can deploy executive sponsors, adjust commercial terms, and rescue the opportunity weeks before quarter-end.
