## Complete LinkedIn Post Copy

'Just checking in to see if you had any questions on my proposal.'

Delete this sentence from your sales vocabulary forever.

When a sales rep sends a 'checking in' or 'bumping this to the top of your inbox' message, they communicate one thing to the buyer:
'I have no value to offer, but I have a monthly quota I need to hit.'

High-performing enterprise professionals follow up with commercial value, not commercial desperation.

Every follow-up communication should advance the buyer's internal thinking through three core principles:

1. Synthesize Agreed Outcomes: Reiterate the specific metric the executive champion agreed was critical during discovery, reminding them of the cost of delay.
2. Deliver Unprompted Value: Share a relevant customer benchmark, an implementation roadmap template, or an industry compliance briefing tailored to their business.
3. Establish a Low-Friction Next Action: Instead of asking 'Do you have 30 minutes to chat?', propose a specific decision milestone: 'I put together the draft security redlines we discussed. Should we align your InfoSec lead on a 15-minute sync this Thursday?'

Follow-up quality is the single best indicator of whether a sales organization is viewed as an annoying vendor or an indispensable partner.

Look at your team's sent folder today. How many 'checking in' emails are sitting there?
