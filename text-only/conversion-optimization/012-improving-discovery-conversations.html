Discovery is not an interrogation. It is a strategic diagnostic.

Most sales reps treat discovery as a qualification checklist:
- 'What CRM are you currently using?'
- 'How many seats do you need?'
- 'What is your budget for this fiscal year?'
- 'When are you planning to make a decision?'

By minute fifteen, the prospect feels like they are being audited. They give terse, guarded answers, and the conversation stays completely surface-level.

High-performing enterprise sellers operate like management consultants. They don't ask basic questions that could have been researched on LinkedIn. They ask catalytic diagnostic questions that illuminate unseen operational risks:

  1. Exploring Secondary Consequences: 'When your sales pipeline stalls at the proposal stage, what downstream impact does that have on your executive hiring plan and board commitments?'
  2. Uncovering Priority Trade-Offs: 'You mentioned three major initiatives this quarter. If you could only solve one without risking quota attainment, which one takes precedence and why?'
  3. Mapping Consensus Roadblocks: 'When you evaluated solutions like this in the past, where did friction typically emerge between your revenue operations and engineering teams?'

When your discovery reveals insights the buyer hadn't fully articulated themselves, you stop being a vendor competing on features. You become a trusted commercial advisor.

Review your reps' top 5 discovery questions. Are they qualifying for the rep's convenience, or diagnosing for the customer's benefit?
