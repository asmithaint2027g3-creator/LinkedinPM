## Complete LinkedIn Post Copy

Missing your sales forecast by 30% is not bad luck. It is a failure of commercial operating discipline.

In many corporate boardrooms, revenue forecasting is treated like a lottery:
- Executive leadership hopes the commit number comes in.
- Reps pull forward unvalidated deals to make their numbers look healthy.
- At quarter-end, the company misses target by $2M, leaving the CFO scrambling to adjust hiring and capital expenditure budgets.

When a company cannot forecast revenue accurately, it loses executive credibility with investors and board members.

Forecasting accuracy is not an analytical trick. It is an operating culture built on three non-negotiable rules:

1. Ban Narrative Selling in Forecast Reviews: If an account executive says 'They told me they love our product and are just waiting on paperwork,' that deal does not belong in the commit. It only belongs in the commit when there is verified written confirmation from the legal or procurement team.
2. Empirical Dwell-Time Penalties: Any deal that exceeds 1.5x the median historical stage duration is automatically discounted or removed from the baseline forecast by the RevOps system.
3. Post-Quarter Forecast Audits: Track individual rep and manager forecast variance every quarter. Hold leaders accountable for forecast accuracy just as strictly as quota attainment.

When forecasting is an empirical operating discipline, variance drops to under 5% and corporate planning becomes bulletproof.
