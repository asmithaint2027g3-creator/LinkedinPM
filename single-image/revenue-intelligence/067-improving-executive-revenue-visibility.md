## Complete LinkedIn Post & Caption

When an enterprise deal slips from June 30 to July 31, reps usually say: 'Legal is just reviewing the redlines. It will definitely close in two weeks.'

Two months later, the deal is still sitting in the pipeline.

Data reveals that 75% of deals that slip once will slip a second time—and over half will ultimately close-lost or go dark.

Deals do not slip because of administrative delays. They slip because of execution gaps earlier in the sales cycle:

1. The Procurement & InfoSec Ambush: The rep waited until the proposal was accepted before asking about security reviews. Procurement suddenly introduces a 6-week vendor vetting process.
2. The CFO Payback Veto: The business case was pitched around soft productivity gains ('saving our team time') rather than hard cash-flow payback.
3. Champion Authority Mismatch: The champion loved the platform, but lacked the political clout to push the contract through internal budget committees.
4. Manufactured Vendor Deadlines: The rep tried to force an end-of-quarter close date without tying it to an agreed customer business event.

Stop treating slipped deals as bad luck. Treat deal slippage as a diagnostic signal that your commercial qualification process broke down.
