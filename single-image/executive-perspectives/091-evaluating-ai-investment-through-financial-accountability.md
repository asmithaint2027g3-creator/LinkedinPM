## Complete LinkedIn Post & Caption

When the sales team announces a new $1M ARR customer, the sales department celebrates.

The CFO's job is to ask: 'What is the real quality of that revenue?'

If that $1M contract:
- Was sold at a 35% discount.
- Requires dedicated custom engineering scripts that drain R&D capacity.
- Was signed on monthly billing arrears terms.
- Has a low retention probability due to poor ICP fit.

That contract is not an enterprise asset. It is a working capital drain that dilutes company valuation.

The CFO Revenue Quality Framework:
1. Gross Margin Contribution: Contracts must generate 80%+ gross margin after factoring in hosting, support, and third-party data APIs.
2. Net Revenue Retention Trajectory: The customer must have a verified expansion vector that compounds contract value year-over-year.
3. Cash Flow Terms: Multi-year agreements must require annual upfront payment to fund working capital without debt or equity dilution.
4. Architectural Repeatability: The platform must deliver value using standard software architecture, zero custom code branches.

Growth is vanity. Revenue quality is enterprise value.

How much of your company's ARR meets high-quality institutional standards?
